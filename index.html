Metadata-Assisted Multi-Label Classification of News Videos.

A research-oriented pipeline for classifying news videos across five semantic levels: Content Format, Source Type, Primary Language, Geography, and Production. The work studies two intentionally separate approaches — a metadata baseline and a visual deep-feature pipeline — rather than combining them prematurely.

01 · Overview

What is the system trying to learn?

The research question

Can news videos be characterized at multiple semantic levels using information available from their online metadata and their visual content?

Rather than treating a news video as a single class, the project predicts five related but independently modelled attributes.

Why two approaches?

The metadata experiment directly consumes YouTube URLs and textual/metadata signals. The final visual pipeline consumes locally stored MP4 clips. Their inputs and processing assumptions are different, so they remain separate in this implementation.

Future multimodal fusion is deliberately left as future work.

Shot segmentation

Frames are sampled with a configurable skip interval and compared through color-histogram distributions. A threshold is used to identify likely shot boundaries.

DB(P,Q) = √(1 − Σi √(PiQi))

Project configuration: 16 histogram bins · frame skip 28 · threshold 0.5.

Representative-frame strategy

For each detected shot, the pipeline selects the middle frame rather than treating every video frame as an independent sample.

kshot = ⌊(fstart + fend) / 2⌋

The resulting keyframes are encoded and mean-pooled into one fixed-size representation per video.

05 · Mathematical Foundations

From text weighting to visual similarity.

TF-IDF — metadata baseline

For a term t in document d, the metadata pipeline uses term frequency and inverse document frequency to weight informative tokens.

TF-IDF(t,d) = TF(t,d) × log(N / DF(t))

Frequent terms within one video receive more weight, while terms occurring across many videos become less discriminative.

Bhattacharyya distance — shot boundaries

Color-histogram distributions from sampled frames are compared to identify visual changes that may correspond to shot transitions.

DB(P,Q) = √(1 − BC(P,Q))
BC(P,Q) = Σi √(PiQi)

Video-level representation

DINOv2 maps each representative keyframe into a 384-dimensional vector. The frame-level vectors are mean-pooled to obtain a fixed representation for the video.

zvideo = (1/K) Σk=1K zk,   zk ∈ ℝ384

Evaluation metrics

For each target, the models are evaluated with accuracy plus weighted precision, recall and F1.

Precision = TP/(TP+FP)
Recall = TP/(TP+FN)
F1 = 2PR/(P+R)

08 · Metadata Baseline

A useful baseline — and a useful limitation.

60%Test accuracy
0.44Macro F1
0.53Weighted F1
58%Cross-validation accuracy

What the baseline established

The metadata experiment demonstrated that title/description/tags and related textual signals can provide useful coarse-grained classification cues. Geography was used as a key early target because class imbalance made the limitations visible.

Why it did not replace the visual pipeline

Metadata is dependent on what a platform exposes and what a publisher writes. It cannot directly observe visual production style, scene composition or visual structure. The visual pipeline therefore became the main/final approach.

13 · Limitations & Future Work

What comes next?

Class imbalance

Rare categories — especially within the 13-class Content Format target — make classification substantially harder and motivate better sampling and imbalance-aware learning.

Audio & speech

Primary Language is difficult to infer visually when language is conveyed mainly through speech. Audio-based language identification and transcription are natural next steps.

Multimodal fusion

A future system could combine metadata, visual features, audio, OCR and on-screen text rather than using the two present pipelines independently.

Larger dataset

More videos and more balanced coverage would make the evaluation more robust and reduce sensitivity to minority classes.

Explainability

Confidence estimation and interpretable evidence could help show why a video was assigned a particular target label.

Deployment

An optional web-based interface could expose the end-to-end inference pipeline without changing the underlying research architecture.
